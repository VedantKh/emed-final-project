# Data Breach & Privacy Crisis: Responding to a Major Cybersecurity Incident

## Background

BlueHorizon Health Insurance has experienced a significant data breach discovered on March 15, 2023. The breach was detected by your cybersecurity team after unusual database query patterns were identified. Forensic investigation reveals that unauthorized access began approximately 47 days before detection. Your company serves 12.5 million members across 28 states and is publicly traded on the NYSE.

## Current Situation

It's now March 18, 2023, and the following details have emerged:

### Breach Scope and Impact

- Protected Health Information (PHI) of approximately 2.3 million members has been compromised
- Exposed data includes: full names, dates of birth, addresses, Social Security numbers, member IDs, and medical diagnosis codes
- Payment information including 840,000 credit card numbers and 1.2 million bank account details used for premium payments was accessed
- Internal emails discussing coverage denial decisions for 76,000 members were exfiltrated
- Preliminary evidence suggests the attackers may have been state-sponsored, with data potentially being sold on dark web marketplaces

### Technical Assessment

- The breach occurred through a vulnerability in a third-party billing system that had a delayed security patch
- Your CISO confirms the vulnerability has now been patched, but cannot guarantee no additional backdoors exist
- Digital forensics is still ongoing, with full scope potentially not known for weeks
- IT estimates 3-4 weeks to implement enhanced security measures across all systems
- Your systems passed a SOC 2 compliance audit just 3 months ago

### Regulatory Obligations

- HIPAA Breach Notification Rule requires notification to affected individuals within 60 days
- State laws in 12 of your operating states require notification within 30 days
- SEC disclosure requirements apply due to the material nature of the incident
- Office for Civil Rights (HHS) investigation is likely, with potential fines up to $1.5 million per violation category
- State attorneys general from 6 states have already requested information about the breach

### Financial Implications

- Estimated immediate costs for investigation, notification, and credit monitoring: $18-25 million
- Potential regulatory fines based on similar breaches: $30-100 million
- Class action lawsuits are expected with estimated liability: $80-200 million
- Stock price has already dropped 6% on rumors before any official announcement
- Cyber insurance policy has a $10 million limit with a $2 million deductible

### Stakeholder Reactions

- Your call centers are experiencing a 40% increase in volume from members who have heard rumors
- Three major hospital systems in your network have requested emergency security reviews
- The Board of Directors has called an emergency meeting for tomorrow
- Media outlets have begun calling your communications team for comment
- Employee morale is suffering with concerns about job security and reputation damage

## Media and Public Perception

Technology and healthcare reporters are actively investigating the story. Several cybersecurity blogs have published preliminary details, some accurate and some exaggerated. Social media monitoring shows growing concern among members, with many expressing anger about potential identity theft risks. Your company's trust score on consumer rating sites has dropped 18 points in 24 hours as rumors spread.

## Decision Points

As CEO, you face critical decisions about disclosure timing, remediation approaches, communication strategy, and organizational accountability during this rapidly evolving crisis.

## Key Stakeholders

- 2.3 million affected members whose data was compromised
- All 12.5 million members concerned about their data security
- Board of Directors and shareholders watching stock value closely
- Executive leadership team, particularly CISO, CIO, and Chief Privacy Officer
- Federal regulators (HHS Office for Civil Rights, FTC, SEC)
- State attorneys general and insurance commissioners
- Healthcare provider partners concerned about their own liability
- Media and cybersecurity analysts scrutinizing your response
