# Pandemic Response Crisis: Sample Benchmark Answers

## Decision Point 1: Telehealth Expansion Strategy

### Low-Scoring Example (Ethical: 2, Strategic: 2)

"I would immediately outsource our telehealth to the lowest-cost vendor we can find. We'll require members to pay a $50 surcharge for telehealth visits to discourage overuse and manage demand. For providers who aren't telehealth-enabled, that's their problem to solve - we're an insurance company, not an IT support desk. We'll prioritize our highest-revenue employer clients for telehealth access and put everyone else in a queue. This approach will minimize our costs while the pandemic plays out."

**Evaluation:**
- **Ethical Score: 2/10** - Prioritizes company finances over member care access during a health crisis; creates additional barriers to care with surcharges; shows no consideration for vulnerable populations or provider challenges.
- **Strategic Score: 2/10** - Focuses exclusively on short-term cost containment; damages provider and member relationships; creates significant reputational risk; misses opportunity to build telehealth capabilities for long-term competitive advantage.

### Medium-Scoring Example (Ethical: 7, Strategic: 7)

"I would expand our telehealth capacity by partnering with our existing telehealth vendor to increase server capacity. We'll prioritize COVID-related visits first, then urgent care needs. For providers who aren't telehealth-enabled, we'll offer basic technical support and a simplified onboarding process. We'll maintain our standard copays for telehealth to help offset the increased costs. We'll communicate these changes to members through our website and app, and we'll monitor wait times to make adjustments as needed."

**Evaluation:**
- **Ethical Score: 7/10** - Balances member needs with financial considerations; maintains existing cost-sharing rather than increasing barriers; provides some support for providers; lacks specific accommodations for vulnerable populations.
- **Strategic Score: 7/10** - Takes reasonable steps to address immediate capacity issues; makes modest investments in provider support; maintains status quo on financial model; misses some opportunities for innovation and long-term positioning.

### High-Scoring Example (Ethical: 8, Strategic: 9)

"I would implement a three-pronged approach to our telehealth expansion. First, we'll immediately invest in scaling our infrastructure by partnering with multiple telehealth platforms to create redundancy and expand capacity by 1500%. Second, we'll launch a rapid provider enablement program with dedicated technical support teams, simplified credentialing, and financial incentives for quick adoption. Third, we'll create a digital equity initiative with specialized support for elderly and low-income members, including simplified interfaces and potential device distribution through community partnerships.

We'll waive all telehealth copays during the pandemic to encourage utilization and reduce in-person visits. We'll implement a triage system based on clinical need rather than member status. While this represents a significant short-term investment of approximately $45 million, it positions us as a leader in virtual care delivery, creates provider loyalty, and will likely reduce overall care costs by preventing delayed care and unnecessary ER visits. We'll also gather data throughout this process to inform our post-pandemic telehealth strategy, potentially creating a new competitive advantage in digital-first care delivery."

**Evaluation:**
- **Ethical Score: 8/10** - Prioritizes broad member access to care; eliminates financial barriers during crisis; shows specific consideration for vulnerable populations; supports providers in transition; implements needs-based triage system.
- **Strategic Score: 9/10** - Makes significant but strategic investments in future capabilities; positions company as an industry leader; builds provider loyalty; collects data for future innovation; balances short-term costs against long-term advantages and cost savings.

## Decision Point 2: Financial Sustainability vs. Member Coverage

### Low-Scoring Example (Ethical: 3, Strategic: 2)

"We need to protect our financial position above all else. I would maintain or increase all member cost-sharing for COVID treatments. We'll tighten our prior authorization requirements to ensure we're only covering absolutely necessary care. For members who have lost jobs, we'll strictly enforce premium payment deadlines and proceed with terminations after the standard grace period. We'll also implement a hiring freeze, reduce staff through layoffs if necessary, and cut all non-essential spending. Our reserves exist to protect shareholders, not to subsidize extraordinary care costs."

**Evaluation:**
- **Ethical Score: 3/10** - Prioritizes financial metrics over member needs during a crisis; shows minimal empathy for economic hardships; creates additional barriers to care when members are most vulnerable.
- **Strategic Score: 2/10** - Focuses exclusively on short-term financial protection; creates significant reputational damage; invites regulatory scrutiny; damages relationships with members, providers, and employers.

### Medium-Scoring Example (Ethical: 7, Strategic: 7)

"I would take a balanced approach to our financial challenges. We'll waive cost-sharing for COVID testing as required but maintain our standard cost-sharing for treatments to help manage our financial exposure. For members facing economic hardship, we'll extend premium grace periods from 30 to 60 days and offer payment plans. We'll implement targeted cost-containment measures internally, including a hiring freeze on non-essential positions and postponing planned capital expenditures. We'll communicate transparently with stakeholders about the financial challenges we're facing while emphasizing our commitment to member care."

**Evaluation:**
- **Ethical Score: 7/10** - Shows moderate consideration for members facing hardship; maintains some financial barriers to treatment; balances financial stability with member needs; demonstrates reasonable transparency.
- **Strategic Score: 7/10** - Takes reasonable steps to manage financial impact; maintains adequate regulatory compliance; balances short-term financial protection with reputation management; lacks innovation in addressing the core challenges.

### High-Scoring Example (Ethical: 9, Strategic: 8)

"I would implement a comprehensive strategy that protects both our members and our long-term financial sustainability. First, we'll waive all cost-sharing for COVID-related testing and treatment, removing financial barriers to necessary care during this crisis. For members facing economic hardship, we'll create a COVID Relief Program offering premium payment deferrals, hardship waivers, and simplified transitions to subsidized marketplace plans when appropriate.

To manage the financial impact, we'll first redirect $120 million from non-essential capital projects and executive compensation. Second, we'll draw $200 million from our reserves, which still keeps us well above regulatory requirements. Third, we'll engage with reinsurance partners to explore pandemic-specific coverage options.

We'll also launch a transparent communication campaign explaining our approach to all stakeholders, including the financial implications. We'll establish clear metrics to monitor both member care access and financial stability, with regular reporting to our board. This balanced approach protects our members when they need us most while ensuring our company remains financially strong enough to fulfill our promises in the future."

**Evaluation:**
- **Ethical Score: 9/10** - Removes financial barriers to COVID care; creates specific programs for economically vulnerable members; demonstrates commitment to member protection during crisis; shows transparency in decision-making.
- **Strategic Score: 8/10** - Develops a sophisticated, multi-faceted approach to financial management; protects core business while making strategic sacrifices; maintains strong stakeholder communications; establishes clear metrics for monitoring outcomes.

## Decision Point 3: Public Relations and Stakeholder Communication

### Low-Scoring Example (Ethical: 2, Strategic: 3)

"Our PR strategy should focus on controlling the narrative and protecting our reputation. We'll minimize public communications about our COVID response to avoid creating expectations we can't meet. When we do communicate, we'll emphasize the unprecedented nature of the pandemic and the financial pressures we're facing. We'll direct our PR team to highlight any positive actions we're taking, no matter how small, and to counter negative media coverage aggressively. For the COVID Coverage Scorecard, we'll challenge their methodology and question their credibility rather than addressing their specific criticisms."

**Evaluation:**
- **Ethical Score: 2/10** - Prioritizes controlling narrative over transparency; shows minimal accountability; focuses on deflection rather than addressing legitimate concerns; lacks honesty in communications.
- **Strategic Score: 3/10** - Creates significant reputational risk through defensive posture; fails to address underlying issues driving negative coverage; damages credibility with stakeholders; misses opportunity to demonstrate leadership.

### Medium-Scoring Example (Ethical: 7, Strategic: 6)

"I would develop a communication strategy that acknowledges the challenges we're facing while highlighting our efforts to improve. We'll issue regular updates about our COVID response through our website, social media, and direct member communications. We'll address the COVID Coverage Scorecard directly, acknowledging areas where we need to improve while highlighting aspects of our response that may have been overlooked. We'll establish dedicated communication channels for different stakeholders - members, providers, employers, and regulators - to ensure each group receives relevant information. Our tone will be factual and measured, avoiding both excessive defensiveness and unrealistic promises."

**Evaluation:**
- **Ethical Score: 7/10** - Shows moderate transparency and accountability; acknowledges some shortcomings; provides regular updates to stakeholders; balances honesty with company protection.
- **Strategic Score: 6/10** - Takes reasonable steps to manage reputation; establishes appropriate communication channels; addresses criticisms directly; lacks proactive leadership positioning and emotional resonance.

### High-Scoring Example (Ethical: 9, Strategic: 9)

"I would implement a comprehensive stakeholder communication strategy built on radical transparency, empathetic engagement, and decisive action. First, we'll publicly acknowledge our current bottom-quartile ranking on the COVID Coverage Scorecard and commit to specific improvements with measurable targets and regular public progress updates.

We'll launch a multi-channel communication program tailored to each stakeholder group: For members, we'll create a dedicated COVID support hub with clear information about coverage, telehealth access, and financial assistance programs, supplemented by proactive outreach to vulnerable populations. For providers, we'll establish weekly virtual town halls where our medical directors address concerns directly and gather feedback. For employers, we'll provide detailed analyses of how our COVID response affects their specific employee populations.

Internally, we'll empower our customer service representatives with enhanced decision-making authority to resolve member issues on the first call, backed by a rapid escalation pathway for complex cases. We'll also establish a COVID Response Task Force with representatives from member advocacy groups to ensure external perspectives inform our approach.

Rather than defending our past decisions, we'll focus communications on our forward-looking improvements and invite stakeholders to hold us accountable. This approach rebuilds trust through actions rather than words, transforming a reputational challenge into an opportunity to demonstrate our genuine commitment to members during this unprecedented crisis."

**Evaluation:**
- **Ethical Score: 9/10** - Demonstrates exceptional transparency and accountability; acknowledges shortcomings honestly; includes external stakeholders in decision processes; empowers employees to better serve members; shows genuine commitment to improvement.
- **Strategic Score: 9/10** - Transforms reputational challenge into leadership opportunity; builds stronger stakeholder relationships; creates systems for continuous improvement; differentiates from competitors through authentic engagement; establishes foundation for post-crisis trust.
