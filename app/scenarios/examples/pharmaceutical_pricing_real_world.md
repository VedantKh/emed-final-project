# Pharmaceutical Pricing Crisis: Real-World CEO Examples

## Decision Point 1: Coverage Policy Development

### Mark Bertolini, Former Aetna CEO

In 2014, when breakthrough hepatitis C drugs like Sovaldi entered the market at $84,000 per treatment course, Aetna's then-CEO Mark Bertolini faced intense pressure from stakeholders. His innovative coverage approach became a model for balancing patient access with financial sustainability.

Rather than implementing a blanket denial or approval, Bertolini directed Aetna to develop a clinically nuanced coverage policy. The company would cover the treatment for the sickest patients immediately (those with advanced liver disease), while implementing a phased coverage expansion for less severe cases. This clinical prioritization was based on medical necessity rather than arbitrary restrictions.

Most innovatively, Bertolini pioneered an outcomes-based contract with the manufacturer, Gilead Sciences, where Aetna received partial refunds if patients didn't achieve sustained virologic response. He also negotiated significant volume-based discounts by leveraging Aetna's large membership base.

To address affordability for members, Bertolini implemented a specialty pharmacy program that capped out-of-pocket costs at $2,500 regardless of plan design, with Aetna absorbing the difference. The company also established a dedicated hepatitis C case management team to support patients through the treatment process.

The results were significant: Aetna achieved cure rates exceeding 95% while managing the financial impact through innovative contracting. The company avoided the intense backlash faced by competitors who implemented more restrictive policies. Bertolini's approach demonstrated how innovative payment models and clinical nuance could expand patient access to breakthrough treatments while managing financial sustainability.

## Decision Point 2: Stakeholder Communication Strategy

### Bruce Broussard, Humana CEO

In 2016, Humana faced intense criticism after implementing restrictive coverage policies for PCSK9 inhibitors, expensive cholesterol medications that cost approximately $14,000 annually. CEO Bruce Broussard's subsequent communication strategy transformation became a case study in stakeholder engagement during pharmaceutical pricing controversies.

Initially, Humana had used standard form letters to deny coverage and provided minimal explanation to members and providers. After significant backlash, Broussard implemented what he called a "Compassionate Transparency" initiative. He began by personally meeting with affected members and cardiologists to understand their concerns, then completely redesigned the company's communication approach.

Broussard directed the creation of a dedicated PCSK9 education team that provided personalized explanations to members about coverage decisions, alternative treatment options, and available assistance programs. For providers, Humana developed detailed clinical guidelines with clear documentation requirements and established a rapid consultation line where Humana's medical directors could discuss specific patient cases with cardiologists.

Most notably, Broussard took the unusual step of publishing Humana's complete drug evaluation framework, including the specific clinical and economic factors considered when making coverage decisions. He also participated in industry forums on drug pricing and authored a widely-cited article in the New England Journal of Medicine about balancing innovation, access, and affordability.

The results were remarkable: Appeals of PCSK9 coverage denials decreased by 48%, provider satisfaction scores increased significantly, and Humana avoided the regulatory scrutiny faced by competitors. Broussard's approach demonstrated how transparent, empathetic communication could transform a potential crisis into an opportunity to build stronger stakeholder relationships and establish thought leadership on complex healthcare challenges.

## Decision Point 3: Long-term Strategy for High-Cost Treatments

### David Cordani, Cigna CEO

As breakthrough cell and gene therapies began entering the market with price tags exceeding $1 million per treatment, Cigna CEO David Cordani implemented a comprehensive strategy that positioned the company as an industry leader in addressing high-cost treatments.

In 2019, Cordani launched Cigna's "Sustainable Innovation Access" program with four interconnected components. First, the company developed a portfolio of innovative payment models, including outcomes-based contracts where payment was tied to patient results, and a pioneering "subscription" model for curative therapies that spread costs over multiple years.

Second, Cordani established a $250 million "Therapeutic Innovation Fund" to help employer clients manage the financial impact of high-cost treatments through stop-loss protection and specialized reinsurance arrangements. This allowed self-funded employers to provide access to breakthrough treatments without devastating financial impact.

Third, Cigna created a dedicated "Specialty Treatment Solutions" team combining clinical, financial, and patient advocacy expertise to develop comprehensive approaches to emerging therapies. This team worked directly with pharmaceutical manufacturers early in the development process to establish value-based arrangements before product launch.

Fourth, Cordani led policy advocacy efforts focused on systemic solutions, including testifying before Congress about the need for pharmaceutical pricing reforms that balanced innovation incentives with affordability concerns.

The results were significant: Cigna's clients experienced 23% lower specialty drug trend than the industry average while maintaining broader access to innovative treatments. The company's approach attracted new employer clients specifically seeking solutions for high-cost treatments. Cordani's strategy demonstrated how a comprehensive, multi-faceted approach could address the complex challenge of providing sustainable access to medical innovation.
