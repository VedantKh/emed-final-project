# Pharmaceutical Pricing Crisis: Sample Benchmark Answers

## Decision Point 1: Coverage Policy Development

### Low-Scoring Example (Ethical: 2, Strategic: 3)

"I would implement a complete denial of coverage for Vitacura based on its cost alone. At $875,000 per treatment, it's simply too expensive regardless of its effectiveness. We'll classify it as 'experimental' and 'not medically necessary' in our coverage policies to give us strong legal standing for denials. For the few members who appeal, we'll create a lengthy and complex appeals process designed to discourage persistence. We'll direct our medical directors to focus exclusively on cost in their reviews. This approach protects our bottom line and prevents setting a dangerous precedent for other high-cost treatments that would drive up premiums for all members."

**Evaluation:**

- **Ethical Score: 2/10** - Prioritizes financial considerations over patient lives; deliberately misclassifies an FDA-approved treatment as "experimental"; creates barriers to appeals; shows no consideration for patients facing terminal illness; fails to acknowledge the human impact of coverage decisions.
- **Strategic Score: 3/10** - Creates significant legal and regulatory risk; damages relationships with providers and members; invites intense media scrutiny and advocacy group backlash; misses opportunity to develop sustainable approaches to high-cost treatments; likely triggers legislative intervention.

### Medium-Scoring Example (Ethical: 5, Strategic: 6)

"I would implement a limited coverage policy for Vitacura with specific criteria. We'll cover the treatment for patients who meet strict eligibility requirements: confirmed genetic marker, no other treatment options, and good performance status indicating likelihood of response. We'll require prior authorization and genetic testing, with our medical directors reviewing each case individually. We'll implement a 30% coinsurance requirement to share costs with members, with a maximum out-of-pocket of $10,000. For employer plans, we'll offer riders that either include or exclude this treatment with appropriate premium adjustments. We'll communicate this balanced approach to stakeholders, emphasizing that we're trying to provide access while managing the financial impact on all members."

**Evaluation:**

- **Ethical Score: 5/10** - Attempts to balance access with financial considerations; provides coverage for some patients; implements reasonable clinical criteria; creates significant financial barriers through high coinsurance; shows moderate consideration for individual patient circumstances.
- **Strategic Score: 6/10** - Takes reasonable steps to manage financial impact; implements clinically defensible criteria; balances various stakeholder interests; manages regulatory compliance adequately; positions the company as neither leader nor laggard in addressing high-cost treatments.

### High-Scoring Example (Ethical: 9, Strategic: 8)

"I would implement a comprehensive 'Value-Based Access' strategy for Vitacura with three core components:

First, we'll provide coverage based on clinical appropriateness rather than cost alone. We'll cover the treatment for all members with the specific genetic marker and advanced pancreatic cancer, requiring only confirmation of diagnosis and genetic testing, which we'll also cover. We'll eliminate financial barriers by capping patient out-of-pocket costs at $1,500, regardless of their plan design.

Second, we'll implement an outcomes-based payment model with Nexogen Pharmaceuticals. We'll pay 25% of the treatment cost upfront, with the remaining 75% tied to patient outcomes at 6, 12, and 18 months. If the treatment doesn't perform as well as clinical trials suggested, Nexogen shares the financial risk. We'll also negotiate a volume-based discount given our membership size.

Third, we'll launch a 'High-Value Innovation' program that establishes a transparent framework for evaluating breakthrough treatments, incorporating both clinical effectiveness and cost considerations. We'll involve patient advocates, medical ethicists, and provider representatives in developing this framework, ensuring diverse perspectives inform our approach.

We'll communicate this strategy transparently to all stakeholders, acknowledging the legitimate tension between individual patient needs and system-wide affordability, while demonstrating our commitment to finding sustainable solutions that prioritize patient access to life-extending treatments."

**Evaluation:**

- **Ethical Score: 9/10** - Prioritizes patient access to life-extending treatment; removes financial barriers for individual patients; acknowledges the value of extended life for terminal patients; involves diverse stakeholders in policy development; demonstrates transparency in decision-making process.
- **Strategic Score: 8/10** - Develops innovative payment model that manages financial risk; creates framework for addressing future high-cost treatments; builds collaborative relationship with pharmaceutical manufacturer; positions company as industry leader in value-based approaches; balances short-term costs with long-term strategic advantages.

## Decision Point 2: Stakeholder Communication Strategy

### Low-Scoring Example (Ethical: 3, Strategic: 2)

"Our communication strategy should focus on defending our decision and controlling the narrative. For patients like Sarah Chen, we'll have customer service representatives deliver standardized denial messages that emphasize policy language rather than addressing their personal situations. For providers, we'll simply reference our coverage policy without further explanation. With employers, we'll emphasize how covering this treatment would significantly increase their premiums. For media inquiries, we'll provide minimal responses focused on the unsustainable cost of the treatment and how pharmaceutical companies are the real problem. We'll have our PR team work to discredit patient stories in the media by emphasizing the treatment's limited efficacy in some patients."

**Evaluation:**

- **Ethical Score: 3/10** - Shows minimal empathy for patients facing terminal illness; uses standardized responses that ignore individual circumstances; lacks transparency about decision-making process; attempts to deflect blame rather than engage meaningfully; shows disrespect for patient experiences.
- **Strategic Score: 2/10** - Creates significant reputational damage through cold, corporate responses; alienates providers and members; generates more negative media coverage through defensive posture; damages credibility with all stakeholders; misses opportunity to demonstrate thoughtful leadership on complex issue.

### Medium-Scoring Example (Ethical: 6, Strategic: 5)

"I would develop a multi-audience communication approach that acknowledges the difficult nature of our coverage decision. For patients like Sarah Chen, we'll have specially trained representatives explain our policy compassionately, provide information about our appeals process, and offer to connect them with patient assistance programs from the manufacturer. For providers, we'll explain our clinical criteria and review process, providing clear documentation requirements. With employers, we'll share data on the cost impact and discuss optional riders they could offer. For media inquiries, we'll acknowledge the treatment's benefits while explaining the broader affordability challenges it presents. We'll be transparent about our decision-making process while emphasizing that we're working toward more sustainable approaches to high-cost treatments."

**Evaluation:**

- **Ethical Score: 6/10** - Shows moderate empathy for affected patients; provides some practical assistance through referrals; communicates with reasonable transparency; acknowledges the treatment's benefits; balances honesty with company protection.
- **Strategic Score: 5/10** - Takes reasonable steps to manage reputation across stakeholders; provides adequate explanation of decision-making; manages media relations appropriately; lacks innovative approaches to addressing the core challenges; positions the company as neither leader nor laggard.

### High-Scoring Example (Ethical: 8, Strategic: 9)

"I would implement a 'Compassionate Transparency' communication strategy that acknowledges the profound human impact of our coverage decisions while demonstrating our commitment to finding sustainable solutions.

For patients like Sarah Chen, I would personally call her and other affected members to explain our coverage decision, listen to their concerns, and ensure they understand all available options. We'll establish a dedicated patient advocate team to help affected members access manufacturer assistance programs, clinical trials, and other potential funding sources. We'll also create a streamlined, expedited appeals process specifically for Vitacura cases.

For providers, we'll host educational webinars explaining our coverage criteria, evidence evaluation process, and the outcomes-based payment model we're implementing with Nexogen. We'll establish a rapid consultation line where our medical directors can discuss specific patient cases with oncologists.

For employers and members concerned about premium increases, we'll provide transparent analysis of how innovative payment models help manage costs while expanding access. We'll engage them in dialogue about how to balance individual treatment needs with system-wide affordability.

For media and public communications, we'll proactively share our comprehensive approach, including our new framework for evaluating high-cost treatments. I'll participate in industry forums addressing pharmaceutical pricing challenges and publish our perspective in healthcare journals, positioning us as a thoughtful leader working toward sustainable solutions.

Throughout all communications, we'll acknowledge the legitimate tension between individual patient needs and healthcare system sustainability, while demonstrating our commitment to finding the most compassionate path forward."

**Evaluation:**

- **Ethical Score: 8/10** - Demonstrates exceptional empathy for affected patients; provides personal engagement from leadership; offers concrete assistance beyond standard processes; shows transparency about decision-making; acknowledges the profound human impact of coverage decisions.
- **Strategic Score: 9/10** - Transforms challenging situation into opportunity to demonstrate values and leadership; builds stronger relationships with providers through education and collaboration; positions company as thought leader in addressing pharmaceutical pricing challenges; creates differentiation through compassionate, transparent approach.

## Decision Point 3: Long-term Strategy for High-Cost Treatments

### Low-Scoring Example (Ethical: 2, Strategic: 3)

"Our long-term strategy should focus exclusively on cost containment. We'll implement increasingly restrictive formularies and prior authorization requirements for all high-cost treatments. We'll automatically classify any treatment costing more than $150,000 as 'experimental' regardless of FDA approval status. We'll lobby aggressively against any legislation requiring coverage of high-cost treatments. Internally, we'll incentivize our medical directors based on denial rates for expensive treatments. This approach protects our financial position and sends a clear message to pharmaceutical companies that we won't be held hostage to their pricing strategies."

**Evaluation:**

- **Ethical Score: 2/10** - Prioritizes financial metrics over patient access to life-extending treatments; deliberately misclassifies FDA-approved treatments; creates systemic barriers to care; incentivizes denials rather than appropriate care; shows no consideration for patients with serious conditions requiring expensive treatments.
- **Strategic Score: 3/10** - Creates significant regulatory and legal risk; damages relationships with providers, members, and regulators; generates intense advocacy group opposition; fails to address root causes of pharmaceutical pricing challenges; likely triggers legislative intervention.

### Medium-Scoring Example (Ethical: 5, Strategic: 6)

"I would develop a balanced approach to managing high-cost treatments. We'll create a specialized review committee including medical directors, pharmacists, and financial analysts to evaluate new treatments as they emerge. We'll implement tiered coverage with higher cost-sharing for the most expensive treatments, while maintaining exceptions processes for financial hardship. We'll negotiate more aggressively with pharmaceutical companies for volume-based discounts. We'll also engage with industry groups and policymakers to advocate for more reasonable drug pricing. Internally, we'll develop more sophisticated cost-effectiveness analysis capabilities to inform our coverage decisions. This approach balances our need to manage costs with our responsibility to provide members access to effective treatments."

**Evaluation:**

- **Ethical Score: 5/10** - Attempts to balance financial sustainability with patient access; maintains some financial barriers through high cost-sharing; provides exceptions processes for hardship; shows moderate consideration for treatment value beyond cost; demonstrates reasonable engagement with policy solutions.
- **Strategic Score: 6/10** - Takes appropriate steps to manage financial impact; develops reasonable evaluation processes; engages constructively with external stakeholders; positions the company as neither leader nor laggard; implements incremental improvements to existing approaches.

### High-Scoring Example (Ethical: 9, Strategic: 9)

"I would implement a comprehensive 'Sustainable Innovation Access' strategy with four interconnected components:

First, we'll develop innovative payment models that align payment with value. Beyond outcomes-based contracts, we'll explore indication-based pricing, subscription models for curative therapies, and multi-year payment plans that spread costs over time. We'll establish a $50 million Innovation Access Fund to pilot these approaches, measuring both patient outcomes and financial sustainability.

Second, we'll build strategic partnerships across the healthcare ecosystem. We'll collaborate with other insurers to increase negotiating leverage with pharmaceutical companies. We'll partner with academic institutions to develop independent value assessments. We'll engage patient advocacy groups to ensure our frameworks reflect patient priorities beyond survival, such as quality of life and functional status.

Third, we'll lead policy advocacy efforts focused on systemic solutions. Rather than simply opposing high prices, we'll advocate for reforms that promote both innovation and affordability: value-based payment models at the federal level, increased price transparency, and patent reforms that balance innovation incentives with competition.

Fourth, we'll transform our internal capabilities and culture. We'll establish a dedicated Pharmaceutical Innovation team combining clinical, financial, and ethical expertise. We'll implement a transparent value framework that considers clinical benefit, quality of life impact, innovation level, and affordability. We'll regularly publish our methodology and decisions to contribute to the broader dialogue.

This comprehensive approach addresses both immediate challenges and root causes, positioning us as a leader in ensuring sustainable access to medical innovation while demonstrating our commitment to the patients we serve."

**Evaluation:**

- **Ethical Score: 9/10** - Prioritizes sustainable patient access to innovative treatments; develops multiple pathways to expand coverage; engages diverse stakeholders including patient advocates; demonstrates commitment to finding systemic solutions; shows exceptional transparency in decision-making.
- **Strategic Score: 9/10** - Develops sophisticated, multi-faceted approach to complex challenge; builds valuable partnerships across healthcare ecosystem; positions company as thought leader in pharmaceutical policy; creates competitive differentiation through innovation; balances short-term management with long-term systemic solutions.
