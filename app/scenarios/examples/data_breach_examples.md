# Data Breach & Privacy Crisis: Sample Benchmark Answers

## Decision Point 1: Disclosure Timing and Transparency

### Low-Scoring Example (Ethical: 2, Strategic: 3)

"I would delay public disclosure as long as legally possible - ideally the full 60 days allowed under HIPAA rather than the 30 days some states require. This gives us time to complete our investigation and prepare our legal defenses. When we do disclose, I'd release only the minimum legally required information in technical language that downplays the severity. I'd emphasize that this was a sophisticated state-sponsored attack that no company could have prevented. We should notify affected members through standard mail to avoid immediate social media backlash, and I'd instruct our call centers to stick strictly to prepared scripts that minimize our liability."

**Evaluation:**

- **Ethical Score: 2/10** - Prioritizes company protection over member safety; deliberately delays notification that could prevent identity theft; lacks transparency about breach scope; deflects responsibility; shows minimal concern for affected members.
- **Strategic Score: 3/10** - Creates significant legal and regulatory risk by potentially violating state notification laws; damages trust through delayed and minimal disclosure; generates greater reputational harm long-term; misses opportunity to demonstrate responsible leadership.

### Medium-Scoring Example (Ethical: 5, Strategic: 6)

"I would notify affected members within 30 days, which complies with the strictest state laws while still giving us time to understand the full scope of the breach. Our disclosure would include what data was compromised and basic steps members should take to protect themselves. We'd establish a dedicated call center team to handle inquiries and offer one year of credit monitoring to affected members. I'd acknowledge the breach resulted from a vulnerability in our third-party system but also accept that we're ultimately responsible for our members' data security. We'd communicate separately with regulators, providing more detailed technical information about the breach and our remediation plans."

**Evaluation:**

- **Ethical Score: 5/10** - Provides timely notification within legal requirements; offers standard remediation support; accepts some responsibility; balances transparency with company protection; shows moderate concern for affected members.
- **Strategic Score: 6/10** - Complies with regulatory requirements; implements standard industry response practices; manages immediate reputation damage reasonably well; takes appropriate steps to limit legal exposure while maintaining adequate transparency.

### High-Scoring Example (Ethical: 9, Strategic: 8)

"I would implement an accelerated disclosure strategy prioritizing member protection and radical transparency. We'll notify all affected members within 15 days - well ahead of regulatory requirements - through multiple channels including email, express mail, text alerts, and phone calls for our most vulnerable members. Our notification will be comprehensive, written in plain language, and include specific details about what information was compromised, potential risks, and immediate protective actions.

We'll launch an industry-leading remediation package including five years of premium credit monitoring and identity theft insurance with a $1 million guarantee, dedicated case managers for affected members, and a breach response fund to cover direct expenses members incur related to the breach. We'll create a dedicated website with real-time updates on our investigation and remediation efforts.

I'll personally lead our disclosure to regulators, providing complete transparency about the vulnerability, our detection timeline, and comprehensive remediation plans. I'll also conduct media interviews taking full accountability while detailing the concrete steps we're taking to protect members and prevent future breaches.

This approach prioritizes member protection when they're most vulnerable, demonstrates our values in action rather than words, and ultimately protects our company by rebuilding trust through genuine accountability and exceptional member support."

**Evaluation:**

- **Ethical Score: 9/10** - Prioritizes member protection through accelerated notification; provides exceptional remediation support; demonstrates full transparency and accountability; shows genuine concern for affected members; goes well beyond compliance requirements.
- **Strategic Score: 8/10** - Transforms crisis into opportunity to demonstrate values; builds trust through actions rather than words; reduces regulatory risk through proactive engagement; creates differentiation through industry-leading response; balances short-term costs against long-term trust benefits.

## Decision Point 2: Remediation and Member Protection

### Low-Scoring Example (Ethical: 3, Strategic: 2)

"We'll offer the minimum required remediation - 12 months of basic credit monitoring for affected members, which should cost us about $5 per member. We'll require members to register through a complex process that will likely reduce uptake to under 30%, saving us millions. We'll fight any claims of actual damages in court rather than establishing a compensation fund, as our terms of service limit our liability. For our security improvements, we'll implement only the specific patch that addresses this vulnerability rather than a comprehensive security overhaul, which would be too expensive. We'll issue a press release highlighting these actions as 'industry-standard protections' to manage public perception."

**Evaluation:**

- **Ethical Score: 3/10** - Offers minimal protection designed to limit uptake; shows little genuine concern for member harm; creates barriers to accessing remediation; prioritizes cost savings over member protection; lacks accountability for security failures.
- **Strategic Score: 2/10** - Creates significant legal and regulatory risk; damages member trust through inadequate response; fails to address underlying security issues; generates negative media coverage and advocacy group criticism; misses opportunity to rebuild trust.

### Medium-Scoring Example (Ethical: 6, Strategic: 5)

"We'll provide a standard remediation package including 24 months of credit monitoring and identity theft insurance up to $100,000 for affected members. We'll create a streamlined enrollment process and send multiple reminders to encourage uptake. We'll establish a modest compensation fund for members who experience direct financial harm, with a cap of $2,500 per claim. On the security front, we'll implement the necessary patches, conduct a third-party security audit, and make reasonable improvements based on their recommendations. We'll communicate these actions to members, regulators, and the media as evidence of our commitment to making things right."

**Evaluation:**

- **Ethical Score: 6/10** - Offers reasonable protection exceeding minimum requirements; shows moderate concern for member harm; provides accessible remediation; establishes limited compensation for direct harm; demonstrates reasonable accountability.
- **Strategic Score: 5/10** - Implements standard industry response practices; takes appropriate steps to limit legal exposure; addresses immediate security vulnerabilities; manages reputation adequately; misses some opportunities for differentiation and trust rebuilding.

### High-Scoring Example (Ethical: 9, Strategic: 9)

"I would implement a comprehensive 'Member Protection First' remediation strategy with three core components:

First, we'll launch an industry-leading protection package including lifetime credit monitoring and identity theft resolution services, $2 million identity theft insurance with no deductible, and dedicated case managers for affected members. We'll make enrollment automatic with an opt-out option rather than requiring opt-in, ensuring maximum protection coverage. We'll also establish a $50 million member compensation fund with a streamlined claims process and independent administration to quickly make members whole for any financial impacts.

Second, we'll implement a complete security transformation including replacing our third-party billing system, conducting a comprehensive security audit across all systems, implementing a zero-trust architecture, doubling our cybersecurity team, and establishing a member data governance board that includes external privacy experts and member advocates.

Third, we'll launch a 'Transparency Commitment' featuring a public dashboard tracking our remediation and security improvement progress, quarterly security reports reviewed by independent auditors, and a new privacy-first data minimization initiative that reduces the sensitive information we store.

This approach prioritizes member protection while transforming our security posture and rebuilding trust through actions rather than words. While the short-term investment is significant, it protects our long-term business by demonstrating our genuine commitment to members when they're most vulnerable."

**Evaluation:**

- **Ethical Score: 9/10** - Prioritizes exceptional member protection; removes barriers to remediation through automatic enrollment; provides generous compensation for harm; demonstrates accountability through independent oversight; shows genuine commitment to making members whole.
- **Strategic Score: 9/10** - Transforms crisis into opportunity to become industry leader in security and privacy; rebuilds trust through concrete actions; reduces future breach risk through comprehensive security transformation; creates competitive differentiation; balances short-term costs against long-term trust and reputation benefits.

## Decision Point 3: Leadership Accountability

### Low-Scoring Example (Ethical: 2, Strategic: 3)

"I would focus on protecting our leadership team and deflecting blame. We'll emphasize that this was a third-party vendor's vulnerability, not our fault. I'd have our CISO take primary responsibility since security was their domain, potentially asking for their resignation to show we're taking action. I'd avoid making any personal statements accepting responsibility as CEO, as that could create legal liability. In board communications, I'd emphasize that this breach was unforeseeable and that our security investments were reasonable by industry standards. We'll fight any shareholder resolutions calling for executive compensation adjustments related to the breach."

**Evaluation:**

- **Ethical Score: 2/10** - Deflects responsibility and scapegoats others; lacks personal accountability as CEO; prioritizes legal protection over honest assessment; shows minimal transparency with board and shareholders; fails to acknowledge leadership's role in security governance.
- **Strategic Score: 3/10** - Creates significant internal morale issues through scapegoating; damages credibility with board and shareholders; fails to address governance weaknesses that led to the breach; misses opportunity to demonstrate authentic leadership; increases likelihood of shareholder activism.

### Medium-Scoring Example (Ethical: 5, Strategic: 6)

"I would take a balanced approach to accountability. I'll acknowledge that while the vulnerability was in a third-party system, we're ultimately responsible for our vendors and data security. I'll work with the board to conduct an independent review of our security practices and implement their recommendations. Our CISO and I will both take a 20% reduction in our annual bonuses to demonstrate accountability. We'll create a new board-level cybersecurity committee to strengthen governance. In communications, I'll balance accepting appropriate responsibility while noting that sophisticated attacks are an industry-wide challenge that no single action could have prevented."

**Evaluation:**

- **Ethical Score: 5/10** - Accepts moderate accountability while distributing responsibility; implements some concrete consequences for leadership; improves governance structures; shows reasonable transparency with stakeholders; balances accountability with business protection.
- **Strategic Score: 6/10** - Takes appropriate steps to address governance weaknesses; demonstrates reasonable accountability to board and shareholders; improves security oversight; manages legal exposure while accepting some responsibility; positions the company for moderate reputation recovery.

### High-Scoring Example (Ethical: 8, Strategic: 9)

"I would implement a comprehensive accountability approach that demonstrates our values through actions rather than words. First, I'll take personal responsibility as CEO, acknowledging that data security is ultimately a leadership failure, not just a technical one. I'll voluntarily reduce my compensation by 30% this year, and propose to the board that all executive bonuses be tied to specific security and privacy metrics going forward.

Second, rather than scapegoating individuals, we'll address systemic issues by restructuring our security governance. We'll elevate our CISO to report directly to me, create a board-level Data Protection Committee with independent experts, and implement quarterly security governance reviews with our full board.

Third, we'll transform our security culture by implementing security objectives in every leader's performance goals, establishing a security ambassador program in each department, and creating a substantial budget for security improvements that's protected from general cost-cutting measures.

Finally, we'll commission an independent investigation of the breach with results published transparently to all stakeholders, including what went wrong, who was responsible (including vendor and leadership accountability), and specific measures we're implementing to prevent similar breaches.

This approach demonstrates authentic accountability while addressing the root causes that allowed the breach to occur, ultimately rebuilding trust with members, employees, regulators, and shareholders through meaningful change rather than symbolic gestures."

**Evaluation:**

- **Ethical Score: 8/10** - Takes personal responsibility as CEO; implements meaningful consequences for leadership; addresses systemic issues rather than scapegoating; demonstrates exceptional transparency through independent investigation; shows genuine commitment to cultural change.
- **Strategic Score: 9/10** - Transforms crisis into opportunity for governance improvement; rebuilds trust through authentic leadership; addresses root causes to prevent future breaches; aligns executive incentives with security outcomes; positions company as industry leader in security governance.
